# Continuity & World-Logic Audit — The Ninth Anomaly

Auditor: continuity pass over `full.md` (18 chapters, read in full) against `story/state/world.md`, `story/state/characters.md`, `story/state/foreshadowing.md`, and `story/chapter_summaries.md`. All quotes verified against `full.md` line numbers.

## Verdict

**The book is in strong structural health, with one genuine hole at the load-bearing point of the plot.** The foreshadowing architecture is unusually disciplined: all eight ledger items (F1–F8) are planted and paid, and the ch.15 memory-burn is fully funded by ch.1–8 plants. However, the climax physically cannot happen without an event no character planned — the inner door requires a clean signature, which defeats the entire deal as the cat sold it in ch.3 — and the text never acknowledges that the cat's twenty-year plan had no answer for the last door. Around that: one flat set-dressing contradiction (the console), one cat-in-two-places timeline break (ch.6 vs. ch.7), and a retainer-rule wobble in ch.1 that undercuts the archive's reason to exist. Everything else is nitpick-grade.

Findings: 1 CRITICAL, 5 MAJOR, 18 MINOR.

---

## CRITICAL

### C1. The cat's plan has no solution for the inner door; the deal as sold in ch.3 was impossible to complete

The entire quest rests on the cat's ch.3 pitch:

> *"Your gates ask for a signature. A living legal citizen, presented voluntarily. They do not ask for a clean one — they ask for a valid one, and you are valid. Marked, locked, stamped, and valid. The only kind of signature this city has ever let through that door."* (full.md:307)

This is false. Ch.13 shows the inner door refuses exactly Arthur's signature class:

> SIGNATURE: MARKED / STATUS: IRREPARABLE / CLEAN SIGNATURE REQUIRED / ACCESS DENIED (full.md:1571–1574)

Arthur himself had only partial documentation — "The archived page had told him the vault kept three doors: codes, then signatures, and then a third, which the page had not named" (full.md:1418) — so *nobody inside the plan* knew what the third door wanted until Vance volunteers the information at the end of ch.11 ("The inner door wants a clean signature. Yours is dirty.", full.md:1422). The mission succeeds only because the antagonist independently converts and brings the one qualifying credential. Ch.14 even restates the broken premise: "It had needed a mind that remembered, **a signature that would pass the doors**, a body the city's eyes would not hold" (full.md:1646) — but the signature did not pass the doors.

This is not a character lying within a plan that works; it is the plan not working, for reasons the cat (a being that "priced the whole history of your grid," full.md:323, and came "to finish what 01–08 could not") should plausibly have known, or at least priced as a risk. The text never asks the question. The fix is cheap — one beat in ch.9 or ch.14 where the cat admits the third door was never in any document it could read and that this door was the plan's unpriced hole — but right now the single most important logistical fact of the climax arrives as a gift from the plot.

**Who wins:** the text (ch.13) over the cat's ch.3 claim — ch.13 is the dramatized ground truth. But the ch.3 line needs to be reframed as the cat's incomplete knowledge, and the hole needs one line of acknowledgment, or the ending is unearned in-logic.

## MAJOR

### M1. The console that doesn't exist (ch.13 vs. ch.17)

Ch.13 describes the deepest chamber with emphatic exclusion:

> "No racks. No glass. No cabinets of the net, **no consoles**. Nothing the city would have called its heart." (full.md:1610)

Ch.17 then uses a console twice as the instrument of the novel's biggest payoff:

> "He turned and put both palms flat on **the console under the pod's open throat**, and he pushed…" (full.md:2005)
> "His hands hung at his sides, loose, **at the height of the console**…" (full.md:2023)

F1's payoff (Arthur braces and stands) is staged on a piece of furniture the entrance inventory explicitly ruled out. Either delete "no consoles" from ch.13 or re-stage the stand on the pod itself (ch.17 already has him grab the pod at line 2011). Note `chapter_summaries.md` ch.17 repeats "braced on the console," so the summary inherits the error.

### M2. The cat confronts Vance in ch.6 while canonically unconscious on Arthur's knees

Ch.6 (Vance POV, log entry time-stamped 06:55, full.md:716): the anomaly stands alone in the dark a hundred metres off, presses on Vance's skull ("It came up against the inside of his skull like water against a sealed hatch," full.md:696), wipes seven seconds from Torelli, and "moved off down the street, deliberately, unhurried" (full.md:704).

Ch.7 opens (later the same morning) with:

> "The cat **had not opened its eyes since the underpass**… It lay stretched and wrong on the blanket on his dead knees…" (full.md:804)

The underpass scene ends ch.5; ch.6 falls between the underpass and the seam gate. In that window the cat is simultaneously (a) standing, looking, walking, and spending power on Torelli in front of Vance, and (b) eyes-shut comatose on Arthur's lap, with Arthur's POV chapters (5 and 7) never registering the encounter at all — for a protagonist whose defining trait is that he notices everything. The Torelli expenditure also goes unbooked in the F2 cost thread, where every other use is meticulously invoiced. A two-line patch in ch.5 or ch.7 (Arthur wakes; the cat is gone; it returns wrong) would close it, or the ch.6 shape can be made explicitly a projection — but as written it is a location/state contradiction.

### M3. The archive is staffed by people who slide — undercutting the retainer premise

Ch.1, two carrels down:

> "Her eyes went soft. Her hands paused one beat. And the beat was gone, absorbed into a blankness that left nothing behind. The tract went back into the chute." (full.md:41)
> "He had seen it happen before, on other faces, at other carrels. For him it had never taken." (full.md:43)

But `characters.md` states retainers "are used as archive sorting labor **precisely because they can perceive forbidden content without auto-erasure**," and ch.1 itself says the floor's workers are "marked, locked" failures of the screening. If the archive's clerks auto-erase forbidden content on contact, the archive cannot function as described ("those who can see are used to bury what they see"), and the scene accidentally demonstrates the opposite of the rule it exists to establish. The charitable reading — the floor warehouses *partial* screening failures, and only true retainers like Arthur can actually read — is coherent but nowhere stated. One clause in ch.1 ("most of them went blank; that was most of why the work backed up" or similar) resolves it. **Text should win** (the blanking is the book's Rule-1 demo), `characters.md` should be amended.

### M4. Sensor detectability is established in ch.2 and ignored in ch.7

Ch.2's whole inciting mechanism is that the grid detects the cat without any mind involved:

> "Heat without an owner. Mass without a citizen number." (full.md:111) — and the officers' "instruments painted a shape" (full.md:127)

Ch.7's brand-new signature gate — built mid-hunt, pods "so new their calibration strips still carried the pale factory film" (full.md:798) — reads Arthur and nothing else while a warm black mass sits openly on his lap, with no illusion spent: "did not spend so much as a shiver of the smoothness… It simply was, and the gate read him, and not it" (full.md:838). The offered mechanism — "a gate that counted signatures had nothing in it to count" (full.md:842) — contradicts the ch.2 premise that the grid flags unsigned mass/heat on its own. A single line (the new gate traded mass-sensing for signature purity, and the cat priced *that*) would pay for the pass honestly. As written, the book's most security-intensive checkpoint is blind to the thing the whole city is hunting.

### M5. The engineer's file is both four days old and "eight years deep"

Ch.1 (day zero, 14:47): "The engineer's file came up at 14:47… He memorized the engineer's format deliberately, which was different from how he memorized everything else" (full.md:57–63) — framed as a singular, recent event the same day the cat arrives.

Ch.4: the rotation tables "sat in the same condemned batch as the engineer's file… He had filed both facts **years ago**" (full.md:472–476).

Ch.13: "The page was where he had left it, **eight years deep**, whole, exact: the engineer's clearance…" (full.md:1539)

The F5 keystone item wobbles between "sorted this week" and "filed years ago" three times. Ch.1 should win — the file's late arrival is the book's inciting coincidence and is load-bearing in ch.3/ch.9 ("the address he had carried for a day," full.md:1050). Fix the ch.4 and ch.13 phrasings.

## MINOR

1. **Ch.4, "twelve hours ago"** (full.md:517): the fugitive alert is 03:14; Vance issued the envelope the morning before (after the 07:20 log entry, full.md:422) — roughly nineteen hours, not twelve. Only works if he sat on the file until ~15:00, which the scene flow doesn't suggest.

2. **Where Arthur read IRREPARABLE.** Ch.1: "His own file had crossed his carrel once, in a batch of his records" (full.md:55). Ch.9: "the word he had read once **over a clerk's shoulder**" (full.md:1130). Two different single occasions for the same formative reading.

3. **Ch.10, the cat's timestamp.** "He signed the order at twenty-three fifty-two" (full.md:1206) — but 23:52 is when *GRANTED* came back (full.md:779–781); Vance signed the requisition earlier that evening. Small misattribution, odd in a book this exact about times.

4. **The "personel" typo / "Question: whose?"** (full.md:390, 424). Vance's log asks outright who wrote the self-closed close-out; the book never answers. Ch.6's "as the close-out had amended itself" (full.md:670) implies the grid self-smooths, but the planted question and its hand-tended typo read like a promise. Either answer it or cut the typo.

5. **Ch.15, "eleven years of his neighbor's dog"** (full.md:1774). Eleven years of dog ends after the Culling (20 years ago) unless the span is pre-Culling — in a city where "the concept of pets does not exist" (world.md #6). Charitable reading works; as phrased it invites the wrong math.

6. **Ch.16's onion and the tailed shape.** "someone was cooking an onion: the oil in the pan" (full.md:1915) and "something crossed the hall at ankle height, quick, with a tail" (full.md:1919) — hours after the broadcast. The vault is a gene bank; a blade of green pushing through a seam is fine, a mature onion and a running animal the same morning are not, unless warren hoarding / surviving vermin is stated.

7. **Ch.6, "The last gate took his retainer"** (full.md:690). Presumably his credentials — but "retainer" is this book's most loaded noun; as written it momentarily says the gate confiscated a clerk.

8. **Arthur's mother is administratively vanished.** The raid entry's whole inventory is "Item: one picture book" (full.md:1442); no adult is processed, detained, or mentioned, and nothing covers who housed an eight-year-old lock subject for the next decade. The burn of her voice in ch.15 makes the absence conspicuous. Likely intentional elision — flagging because the book is otherwise meticulous about paperwork.

9. **The blanket prop evaporates.** Packed with ceremony in ch.4 (full.md:456), the cat rides it through ch.8 ("He packed the blanket, and they pushed on," full.md:1022), then it is never mentioned again — not when the chair dies (ch.10), not in the crawl. Its *weight* is burned in ch.15 (full.md:1798); the object itself is untracked.

10. **Orphaned setup: the folded breakfast pack.** "He did not know when the folding had become obligatory" (full.md:25), ritually noted, inspected and judged by the cat (full.md:79) — then never paid off or revisited. Reads like a planted compulsion that lost its scene.

11. **Semi-orphaned setup: the ochre-and-soot painted cat** (full.md:632–636). Ch.5 builds it as "the only image in the city that could not be rewritten"; the ch.16 crowd scene revisits the FEATHER & FUR sign (full.md:1917) but not the painting. Thematically complete enough to survive, but it was framed as a promise.

12. **Construction speed.** The seam gate — archway, scanner pods, painted seam — is being finished "since the flat, since the night" (full.md:802), i.e., within roughly two days of the inciting event, wet paint included. Totalitarian efficiency, but fast.

13. **"Exactly one clean signature left in the city"** (full.md:1491) vs. world.md #4's plain "unmarked, clean." The text's implied logic (severance amends the signature, so "clean" = never touched) makes Vance's uniqueness just barely defensible, but it is never stated — under world.md's wording, millions of ordinary unmarked citizens could open the inner door and the climax's key is not rare. **Text should win; add the defining line** (e.g., the door wants a signature the city never amended, and the city amended all of them but his).

14. **The link dies twice.** Ch.10 is absolute: "the link was gone, and the space where the link had been was full of the city's sound" (full.md:1260); ch.13: "gave back nothing" (full.md:1578). Ch.14 then runs a long, syntactically complete telepathic conversation (full.md:1638–1708) before the link "went out: not guttering, emptied" (full.md:1722). The revival is dramatized as a flicker, but "gone" was unqualified; the ledger's description of ch.14 as "final fragments" (foreshadowing.md F2) under-records what the chapter actually does.

15. **The lock's release mechanism is split between two explanations.** Ch.9: "only an interface of the family can release the binding" (full.md:1128). Ch.15 implies the cat does it at closing ("he had given him legs. The rest is at closing," full.md:1808). Ch.17 frames the dead hiss as the net dying ("the silence had the shape of the thing that had made it… the radio was off," full.md:1969). Any one reading works; the book never commits.

16. **Twenty years of disuse, standing in minutes.** Paralyzed from age eight, calves "that had never been taught" (full.md:2003) bear full weight within moments and hold for a morning (ch.17–18). Ch.18's failed first step (full.md:2051) is a good nod to realism, but the physiology is pure fairy-tale. Acceptable as genre convention; noted for completeness.

17. **"Twenty years of clean shelves had never stocked a swear"** (full.md:2013). Arthur spends his working life reading confiscated contraband; that he has no swear word available is a good joke that doesn't survive one carrel of the Archive's intake.

18. **"Stand" used loosely for a paralyzed man** — "the coldest air he had ever stood in" (full.md:532), "the letters he had to stand in front of" (full.md:576). Probably an intentional quiet use of the book's hoarded word (ch.11's "the word *stand* in the lowered drawer," full.md:1354); flagged only in case it isn't.

## State-file drift notes

- **characters.md — "First true sound ch.14–15, once only"**: contradicted by the epilogue, where the cat purrs again (ch.17 full.md:2037, "The key was spent; what remained was a low, even sound"; ch.18 full.md:2065). The text is self-consistent (the *key* is spent once; the *sound* becomes ordinary), so **text wins** — amend characters.md to "true sound first at ch.14–15 as the key; ordinary purr thereafter."
- **"Felix" never appears in full.md** (grep: zero matches). The text calls it only "the cat / the black thing / it." Fine as an off-page name — but if the name is meant to exist in-world, no scene confers it. Note for a decision, not an error.
- **characters.md — retainers as archive labor**: see M3. The ch.1 blanking scene contradicts "without auto-erasure." Text wins; amend characters.md to distinguish full retainers (Arthur-grade) from partial screening failures shelved alongside them.
- **characters.md / world.md #2 — "~1/10,000"**: the ratio never appears in the text, and the cat calls Arthur "the only one in this city who has ever looked at me and kept looking" (full.md:311) despite a floor full of marked clerks. Hyperbole, but the state file's precision and the text's rhetoric should be reconciled (e.g., retainers perceive; only Arthur *kept the receipt* whole — which is in fact what the line says).
- **world.md #4 — inner door**: state file says only "unmarked, clean." The text adds "military grade" coloring (full.md:1594) and a uniqueness claim (full.md:1491). Text wins; world.md should define "clean" as never-severed/never-amended or the climax's key is not rare (Minor 13).
- **world.md #6 — "stroke virtual haptic feedback"**: ch.1's bench man strokes bare air with "a remembered animal under his hand" (full.md:67); the haptic band debuts ch.5. Not a contradiction (body-habit vs. product), but the state file's phrasing doesn't cover the book's first use of the motif.
- **chapter_summaries.md — ch.6**: "the net returns nil twice" — the text has one nil that Vance *reads* twice (full.md:642). Micro-drift.
- **chapter_summaries.md — ch.17**: "braced on the console" inherits the M1 contradiction; will need editing with whichever way M1 is fixed.
- **chapter_summaries.md otherwise verified chapter-by-chapter against the text** — times (01:00/01:48/03:09/23:52/00:41), log numbering (3-0711…3-0716; 1-0409/1-0410/1-0411), the unreadable-entries count, the burn order, and the F-tags all match the chapter text. No other drift found.
