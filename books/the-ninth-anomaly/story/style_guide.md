# Style Guide — The Ninth Anomaly

- **Language**: English prose. Past tense. Close limited third person.
- **POV**: Arthur by default. Ch.6, Ch.12, and Ch.15 are full Vance chapters; Ch.4 is half Vance / half Arthur.
- **Tone**: cold, restrained, dense with sensory detail. The cat's scenes permit sardonic brightness.
- **Arthur chapters**: precise interiority; archivist's taxonomic habit (he files sensations, dates, people); long-coiled sentences that snap shut. He never lies.
- **Vance chapters**: visibly different voice — short sentences, numbered thinking, excerpts from his handwritten paper log embedded in the chapter text (set off as block quotes or distinct lines).
- **The cat's dialogue**: ALL italic telepathy (*like this*), sardonic, broker-like. No quotation marks for it. Before ch.14 it must never produce a real audible sound — mime expressions only (yawns, slow blinks, tail flicks described as gestures of mockery).
- **Cat cost progression** must be visible on the body: dull fur (early) → longer sleeps (ch.5) → sluggishness (ch.8) → translucent edges (ch.8–10) → coupling layer burned, near death (ch.10 onward).
- **World texture to weave in, not dump**: no birds; synth food without scent layers; children without the word "furry"; haptic petting of empty air; patrol rotations; signage of the old city only Arthur can read.
- **Genre**: literary dystopian SF. Avoid web-novel tics; no stat blocks; no on-the-nose theme statements except the two sanctioned lines (Vance's "Order is not because I don't remember..." and the final "He had forgotten the word. His hands had not.").
- **Chapter length**: 2,600–3,000 words; ch.14 (merged Vault + Frequency) ≈ 5,500; the single epilogue ch.16 (Morning, merged from the former ch.17 + 18) ≈ 3,600.
- End every chapter on the hook specified in the outline. Do not resolve a hook early.

## Readability contract (added after ch.1–9 review — applies to all new chapters and revisions)

The archivist interiority is a *voice*, not a blanket. Prose must stay readable as a novel first, literary object second:

- **Paragraph cap**: ~6 lines. Long unbroken blocks are reserved for one or two deliberate pressure moments per chapter.
- **Aphorism budget**: at most one or two aphorism-grade sentences ("the silence was not an absence but an announcement") per page. They are spent on real beats, never on routine narration.
- **Dialogue/action minimum**: every chapter needs at least 2–3 beats of dialogue or concrete external action; no chapter may run as uninterrupted interior monologue. Chapters with another character present must let that character act and speak.
- **Opening contract**: within the first ~500 words something must *happen* or *be concretely wrong* — an event, an intrusion, a decision — not just atmosphere.
- **Archive-voice rationing**: the filing/classifying idiom (filed under, unclassified, the account, no record) is a character tic — cap ~3–5 uses per chapter, placed where classification is the point.
- **Sentence rhythm**: vary length aggressively; a long coiled sentence must be followed by short plain ones. Default to concrete verbs over abstract nouns.
- **Show the rule through event, not essay**: world rules land through things happening to people, with explanation compressed to a line or two.
