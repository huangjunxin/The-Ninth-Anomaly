# Characters — The Ninth Anomaly

## Arthur (protagonist)
- 28, clerk in the Forbidden Archive. Marked at age 8 for looking at a picture book of real cats; neural lock installed. Wheelchair user; the lock is a cage, not a treatment. File stamped "irreparable."
- He is a **mnemonic retainer** (~1 in 10,000): the concept–emotion severance does not take on him. Full retainers (Arthur-grade) are used as archive sorting labor precisely because they can perceive forbidden content without auto-erasure — "those who can see are used to bury what they see." The same floor also holds partial screening failures — marked clerks who blank on that content on contact (ch.1), which is most of why the archive's intake backs up; the blanking is the book's Rule-1 demo, and the text wins over any reading where every clerk reads whole.
- Voice signature: precise interiority, an archivist's taxonomic habit (he classifies everything). **He never lies** — "a lie has to be remembered, and I remember too much."
- Desire: to stand / be free (surface); to be acknowledged by the world as a whole person (deep). Fear: that everything he remembers means nothing.
- Arc: carried by the cat → walks to the core on his own knowledge and will → gives his memories back to the world.

## Felix / "It" (the cat)
- Note: "Felix" is this file's off-page name — the novel text never uses it (grep: zero matches); the text calls it only "the cat / the black thing / it."
- High-dimensional genetic intelligence left over from the old civilization; cat form; bio-electric hybrid coupling vessel.
- Manner: mocking, proud, unpredictable, negotiates like a broker quoting prices.
- Abilities (strictly perception/memory layer): cognitive erasure, sensory illusion. Each use costs life force (fur loses sheen → sluggishness → edges go translucent). Useless against military-grade implant firewalls. EMP burns its reality-coupling layer.
- Goal (its own motivation): release the old world's life frequencies back into the human network at the Seed Vault. It needs Arthur's legal citizen neural signature for the biometric gates; Arthur needs it to unlock his neural lock. Each needs the other.
- Blind spot: genuinely cannot understand why humans voluntarily surrendered memory for safety. Arc: by the end it "learns" (stops mocking).
- **Never makes a real sound in Acts 1–3** — only silent mime expressions. Reason revealed ch.9 (purr = vault key frequency, not to be wasted). Its one true sound is the unspent key, spent exactly once, at ch.14: throat pressed to Arthur's Adam's apple, the first true sound of its life *begins* the broadcast (the key is spent once; the sound itself then becomes ordinary). After the key is spent, the purr returns as an un-keyed, everyday sound — the epilogue ch.16 notes it explicitly: "The key was spent; what remained was a low, even sound," kept up for no price and no reason. (This amends the older "once only" phrasing: the text decides the *key* is once-only; the epilogue purr is just a purr.)

## Captain Vance (enforcer / mirror)
- Police unit captain; survivor and executor of the Culling (20 years ago). He led the raid that confiscated a child's picture book and **signed the neural-lock order on Arthur**.
- Coldly rational; does not trust his own brain; keeps **handwritten paper logs** (firewalls can't edit paper). Military-grade implant firewall — the cat's powers do not work on him.
- He is Arthur's inverse answer: he also remembers, and chose order. Thesis line: "Order is not because I don't remember. It's because I do."
- Arc: embodiment of order → broken open by his own old log → opens the last door with his clean signature → walks into the crowd and remembers with them. Not overthrown — he steps down himself.
