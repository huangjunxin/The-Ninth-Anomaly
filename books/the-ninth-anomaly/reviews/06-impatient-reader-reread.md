# Impatient Reader Re-Read — *The Ninth Anomaly* (revised, 16 chapters)
**Persona: impatient genre reader. Comparing the revised text against my original drop-off report (reviews/02-impatient-reader-dropoff.md).**

---

## Verdict Up Front

**Would I finish it now? Yes — and this time I wouldn't white-knuckle it.** The revision fixed my single worst complaint (old Ch 12), broke up the 3-chapter epilogue into one bearable chapter, and — the biggest craft win — moved the price reveal out of old Ch 9 into the merged Ch 14, where the cat's warning "you will hate me" finally *delivers actual hatred* instead of the old version's cop-out ("he could not hate it"). The book still opens too slow and Chapter 4 is still a man doing paperwork, so the two front-loaded quit-points are still live. If a reader survives to Chapter 2's doorframe apology, they now make it to the end. Getting them to Chapter 2 remains the book's unsolved problem.

## Original-List Verification

### Drop-off #1: Ch 1 first half — "nothing is happening and the narrator is showing off" → **STANDING (micro-improved)**
The structure is untouched: warm sill (great hook) → "*Unsourced. No action.*" (the narrator still tells me not to care) → pages of routine → cat appears two-thirds in. My quoted offender is still there verbatim: *"He counted out his gratitude, because gratitude was work and he did it daily."* Small trims only: the redundant "and he did not let it land" is cut from the primer scene (the full word-suppression scene still lands later), and "so he did not think" became "so he kept them out." Net: ~3 lines leaner. The 3-paragraph rule still barely passes on the strength of "The sill was warm," and the sample-chapter problem is unchanged.

### Drop-off #2: Ch 4 entire — "POV switch to a guy doing paperwork" → **STANDING (with a retroactive upgrade)**
Nearly word-for-word the same: "One. Two witnesses. / Two. One hole. / Three. The hole had edges." The coffee, the paper manifesto, the log entries — all present. What changed is downstream: the typo beat ("*personel*, where the manual kept two n's") now **pays off in Ch 9**, where the cat reveals it priced the human clerk who wrote the close-out. That retroactively makes Ch 4's fussiest detail load-bearing. Doesn't make the chapter faster, but it makes it reread-proof. Still the first full stall.

### Drop-off #3: Ch 12 entire — "I already know all of this" → **FIXED (mostly)**
This is the revision's best surgery. Cut roughly in half:
- The journal now quotes only the raid entry; the "heat today is unpermitted" paragraph and the separate lock-order entry (old 1-0410) are condensed into one narrated paragraph ("Past the raid entry stood the test that had proved it...").
- The entire second half is gone: no wafer-key inventory, no "The direction of his boots was down," no closing "One. … Five. Let it open." checklist.
- New ending: *"He did not put it back on the shelf. He did not stand up from the chair. The night went on making its permitted noises, and he sat with the book in his hands, and the drawer stayed shut."*

The chapter now ends on suspended decision instead of a pre-announced march, which makes Vance's arrival at the third door in Ch 13 an actual event. It still sits between Ch 11's cliffhanger and the door — the interruption problem is inherent — but at half the length I'll allow it. One new wrinkle introduced: see "drawer/wafer" in New Problems below.

### Drop-off #4: Ch 7 middle — "the gate scene that never ends" → **SLIGHTLY IMPROVED, STANDING**
The four-other-people procession (haptic woman, cable laborer, scar man, turned-away old man) is fully intact, so the mid-chapter sag remains. What got cut: the cat's wound-up waking re-description (now just "It woke as it woke now" — good, that sentence was on its fifth copy-paste) and the final paragraph ("He was a row with attention on it now…"), so the chapter now ends cleanly on "The name had been the terms all along." A trim, not a fix.

### Drop-off #5: Old Ch 16–18 — "the book ended and kept going" → **IMPROVED (halved)**
Three chapters of denouement are now two. Old 17+18 merged into Ch 16 "Morning," with the worst self-admiration cut: the "muscles that had never been taught stood to the work as if they had been waiting all their lives to be asked" anatomy lesson is compressed to "The calf, the knee, the thigh, the hip — and the stack held," and the abstract essay about the cat learning to take what it's given (old Ch 18's "Giving was not a transaction…" passage) is gone entirely. **New beat added:** the empty-pack fold — "The why of the fold was gone from him; the hands kept the shape without being asked" — a Ch 1 callback that does the chapter's whole thesis in two lines. Old Ch 16 (now Ch 15) is also trimmed: the crowd montage loses the ankle-height cat crossing and the sleeve-touch, and *gains* the best new image in the back half: a second painted cat begun beside the first — "Half an arch. The hook coming back." Still slow, still tone-poem, but it's a 20-page cool-down now instead of 40.

### Worst-momentum chapter: old Ch 6 — "same deductions, now with a field trip" → **STANDING (barely touched)**
Opening folded into one paragraph instead of two, otherwise identical: same "One. The grid amends. / Two. Minds take the cut. / Three. Paper holds." list, same descent re-treading Ch 5's cold and dark. The EMP requisition still does all the work in the last 15 lines.

## Newly Introduced Problems (fresh-read honesty)

1. **Ch 10's opening now front-runs withheld information.** The revised Ch 9 deliberately has Arthur shut the box *before* learning the price ("he shut it by long habit, before the page could show him what stood inside it"). Then Ch 10 opens: *"He had been sitting with the price of the broadcast: the door still sealed, the collar still seized… and the price had not changed."* What price? He doesn't know it yet — that's Ch 14's whole reveal. The colon half-covers it by redefining "price" as door logistics, but I stumbled. One-word fix territory, but it's a real seam from moving the reveal.

2. **The drawer/wafer continuity gap (Ch 12 → Ch 13).** New Ch 12 ends emphatically: *"the drawer stayed shut"* — the symbolic non-decision. Then in Ch 13, Vance "put his hand inside his coat and brought out the wafer," a key the revised text never shows him taking (old Ch 12 had a whole scene of him removing it from that drawer). The symbol says he didn't decide; the next chapter silently proves he did. As a commuter reader I flipped back half a page looking for the moment. It's the one place the revision's cuts left a visible scar.

3. **Ch 13's third-door beat is now pre-spoiled by Ch 9.** The cat's new Ch 9 speech spells out that the last door needs "the one hand it never amended… There is exactly one of it left," and Arthur confirms: "'At him… You went to look at him.'" Good for plot logic (Vance's arrival is no longer a coincidence), but it costs Ch 13 twice: the "ACCESS DENIED" refusal lands as confirmation instead of shock, and Arthur's line there — *"Arthur understood in the same instant… what the third door had been built to admit: one kind of hand"* — is now redundant. He was told. I was told. The text is re-realizing a realized thing.

4. **Ch 9's cat speech is an exposition brick.** Three long italic paragraphs of door mechanics (1138–1144 in the current text), right in what used to be the book's cleanest payoff chapter. I skimmed the middle of it — and I'm the guy who notices when the bookkeeping metaphor recurs. The information is necessary (it sets up Ch 13 and pays off Ch 4's typo), but it's delivered as a lecture where the old chapter was all discovery.

5. **Ch 14's "Not her" passage is pronoun-slippery.** "'Not her,' he said… The price could take the shelf. It could not take her." Then the broadcast takes the mother's voice anyway, keeping only the count ("leave him the ten"). It resolves — "her" means the count, not the voice — but on a first fast read, "could not take her" followed by "He would never know again who had taught him" reads like a broken promise. I re-read the passage twice to catch the distinction. The *idea* is excellent (negotiating the terms of his own spending is the chapter's best new beat); the referent needs one more finger-hold.

6. **Small losses in the trim.** The Ch 1 callback of the bench man stroking empty air is cut from the broadcast sequence — that was my proof the wave reached the upper city, and I miss it. Ch 15 now ends "Vance held the salute." instead of the old "Not for order." — the kicker was the button on the whole chapter and it's gone. Also: the "read it twice" tic has spread to Arthur (Ch 9 and Ch 10 both, with Ch 10 doubling it in consecutive paragraphs: "he had learned to read terrible things twice" then "He read it twice, and the order stood"). The author fixed Vance's screensaver and installed a copy on Arthur.

## Remaining Drop-off Risks, Ranked

1. **Ch 1, unchanged (highest risk, because it's the sample).** The gratitude-passage sag still sits between the hook and the cat. Revision didn't touch the real problem: the book still spends its first six phone screens on routine.
2. **Ch 4, unchanged.** First full stall, now slightly redeemed by the typo payoff — but a first-time reader doesn't know that yet.
3. **Ch 4–6 Vance block.** Still three chapters where the office scenes outnumber the forward-motion scenes ~3:1. The EMP grant remains the only engine in that stretch.
4. **Ch 9's exposition brick** (new entry) — the restructured chapter trades a defused ending (old problem) for a lecture in the middle (new problem). Net positive, but it's a fresh skim-point.
5. **Ch 16 (merged ending).** Still slow by genre standards — but at half its old length and with the pack-fold beat, it now earns patience instead of spending it.

**Bottom line:** the revision removed the quit-worthy chapter (old 12) and the bloated epilogue (old 17–18), and made the climax structurally better. What remains are the book's original front-end sins (Ch 1, Ch 4) — the revision fixed the middle and end and left the front door exactly as sticky as it was.
